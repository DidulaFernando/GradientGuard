\begin{abstract}
Early model measurements may help prioritise classifiers for later subgroup-failure audits despite healthy source performance. GradientGuard summarises the allocation of absolute weight-mean-gradient products across network blocks during the first ten percent of training. Analytical and numerical checks establish measurement correctness. An exploratory outcome pilot trained 96 classifiers in 24 setting families. Its source-normal held-out cohort has 36 classifiers and 12 failures: six non-slope layer summaries raise average precision from 0.775 to 0.934, but paired-family ranking intervals include zero improvement and the original nine-audit discovery comparison is tied. A subsequent matched structural-control analysis reuses those outcomes and yields only a panel-dependent increment. A separately frozen fresh-family validation planned 256 classifiers in 64 new families; 248 completed and eight were unavailable because fixed source constructions failed generator validation. Among 106 source-normal evaluation classifiers with 34 failures, a rich structural predictor and the same predictor augmented with six sensitivity shares both discover 26 failures in 35 audits. Their average-precision difference is 0.0005 (95\% paired-family interval -0.0057 to 0.0096); AUROC and Brier differences also include zero. Smaller-control and secondary-capacity gains remain, while the sensitivity-specific primary benefit is not demonstrated. The contribution is a reproducible, benchmark-specific evaluation with explicit structural controls and a bounded negative validation result.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\label{sec:intro}
Aggregate accuracy can conceal large differences between subpopulations. A classifier that performs well on common combinations of object and background may still fail on less common combinations, making source performance alone an incomplete guide to robustness [1, 2, 3]. When many candidate models are being trained, a developer may have capacity to investigate only a subset in detail. This creates a model-level decision: which apparently healthy runs should receive a later subgroup audit?

We study whether the allocation of early connection sensitivity across network layers provides additional information for that decision. For a frozen model, connection sensitivity is the absolute product of a learned weight and its mean data-loss gradient. Its mathematical primitive is established in pruning and zero-cost model assessment [4, 5]. A global total records the magnitude of this sensitivity; a normalised layer profile records where it is allocated. These quantities can differ even when model outputs and total weight sensitivity agree. This distinction motivates a predictive experiment, rather than demonstrating that layer profiles encode subgroup reliance.

The operational research question is: \emph{Among classifiers that appear healthy early in training, does the allocation of connection sensitivity across layers improve prioritisation for terminal subgroup-failure audits beyond conventional telemetry, global sensitivity and matched layer-wise structural statistics?} In simple terms, we ask whether knowing where a model's loss is sensitive helps choose which models to inspect. This is local sensitivity to multiplicative weight changes on a specified source probe, not a causal account of visual feature reliance. ``Hidden'' refers to unavailable terminal group outcomes at the early decision point; audit groups are predefined.

This article reports a staged investigation. Measurement feasibility and a targeted probe/calibration follow-up precede an exploratory 96-classifier outcome pilot and post-hoc capacity/slope diagnostics. The original pilot designated L8 and 25\% audit capacity as primary. Its L6 and one-third-capacity observations informed a subsequent reused-outcome structural-control study and then a separately frozen fresh-family validation. The latter tests a matched structural pair at one-third capacity; it does not retrospectively replace the pilot endpoint. We report both the original favourable observations and the completed validation that did not establish the sensitivity-specific primary benefit.

The pilot layer variants find ten failures at twelve inspections versus eight for conventional/global predictors, but the original nine-audit result is tied and family uncertainty is substantial. In the fresh validation, the rich structural predictor M and its sensitivity-augmented counterpart MZ each find 26 of 34 failures at 35 inspections. Thus the pilot motivates a hypothesis that the stronger test does not support at its predefined primary decision. This negative result narrows the claim from a sensitivity-specific forecasting advance to a reproducible evaluation of a candidate representation.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item We provide an auditable measurement and experimental pipeline separating implementation correctness, measurement reliability and outcome prediction.
\item We report conventional, global-sensitivity, norm-product and rich structural controls under matched early information, family folds, preprocessing, learner classes and tuning allowances.
\item We combine frozen-prediction ablations, paired-family ranking intervals and a fresh-family matched validation, retaining ties, losses, unavailable constructions and the unmet sensitivity-specific primary gate.
\end{enumerate}
The contribution is an empirical investigation of a candidate representation, rather than invention of connection sensitivity or a demonstrated general-purpose shortcut detector.

\section{Related Work and Information Boundaries}
\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Prediction from early training information}
Naik \emph{et al.} [6] use early loss, accuracy, gradient signal-to-noise, weight-norm growth, activation saturation and hyperparameters to predict training outcomes. This establishes a substantial conventional-information baseline for any proposed internal feature. Monin [7] studies prospective prediction of persistent out-of-distribution degradation from source-side dynamics. The latter's event-conditioned trajectory setting differs from a single fixed early prediction of a completed classifier's subgroup-audit outcome, but changing the target alone does not establish methodological originality.

SPARE [2] identifies early output structure associated with spurious correlations and uses importance sampling for mitigation. Our bounded class-conditioned probability-clustering comparator adapts a discovery signal to model-level forecasting; it does not reproduce SPARE's complete algorithm. In particular, this proof of concept uses probability vectors, whereas the relevant published experiments also use logit-based signals. Prediction depth [8] is another useful comparator, but is not implemented here. Accordingly, we do not claim improvement beyond prediction depth or beyond a full reproduction of these prior systems.

\subsection{Sensitivity and layer statistics}
SNIP [4] supplies the connection-sensitivity primitive. Zero-cost proxies [5] already aggregate related scores for eventual model-performance ranking. SSNR/T-CET [9] explicitly studies saliency composition and layer-aware aggregation, while ZiCo [10] uses gradient-variation information. Early sensitivity and the observation that aggregation can discard layer information therefore have clear prior art.

Weight-distribution statistics also predict network accuracy [11]. Bohadana, Schneider and Katz [12] investigate performance prediction using weights and gradients, close technical prior art for internal histories. We do not reproduce their full temporal predictor. We instead implement explicit norm-product and parameter/mean-gradient marginal controls and compare each structural bank with and without the same six sensitivity shares. The fresh matched result does not establish sensitivity-specific superiority over these controls.

\subsection{Auditing and generalisation measures}
Subgroup discovery and model selection use information regimes different from ours. Domino [13] identifies systematic error slices, and SubpopBench [3] highlights ordinary worst-class information as a relevant robustness signal. Our early features use ordinary source labels; group annotations are required for historical terminal outcome labels and later evaluation. The system neither discovers arbitrary new groups nor eliminates the need for group-labelled audits.

Studies of generalisation measures [14, 15] motivate preserving configuration structure instead of interpreting pooled correlations as universal mechanisms. In our design, the splitting and uncertainty unit is the setting family. Different random seeds within a family, alternative probe panels and overlapping capacity points are not independent experimental settings. These distinctions are central to interpreting the positive signals reported below.


\subsection{The precise gap and candidate contribution}
The distinguishing question is conditional and decision-specific: does a small allocation representation improve the ranking of source-normal completed runs after the information already supplied by ordinary training telemetry and global sensitivity is considered? This differs from proposing another saliency formula. The derivative, normalisation and descriptive summaries are inherited operations; the candidate methodological contribution is their fixed, low-dimensional use in a carefully separated model-audit forecasting task.

Table 1 states the information boundary relative to close prior work; distinct targets alone do not establish superiority. Historical audited models remain necessary for predictor training. Three claims are evaluated separately: useful layer information beyond conventional/global predictors, coordinate-product sensitivity beyond simpler structural statistics, and operational value relative to monitoring cost. The first has some secondary evidence, the fresh primary specificity comparison is tied, and the third remains unestablished.

\section{Connection Sensitivity and Audit Prediction}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Frozen-weight measurement}
For labelled source probe $P=\{(x_j,y_j)\}_{j=1}^{n}$ and frozen weights $\theta$, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
L_P(\theta)&=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^{n}\ell(f_\theta(x_j),y_j),\\
g_i&=\frac{\partial L_P}{\partial\theta_i},\qquad a_i=|\theta_i g_i|.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sensitivity}
\tag{1}\end{equation}
A gate $m_i$ multiplying one weight gives $\partial L/\partial m_i=\theta_i g_i$ at $m_i=1$. For an entire block, however, a common gate yields the \emph{signed} sum $\sum_i\theta_i g_i$, not the sum of absolute sensitivities. The implementation and finite-difference checks preserve this distinction.

For microbatch $b$ of size $n_b$, the correct probe gradient is
\begin{equation}
g=\sum_b\frac{n_b}{n}\,\nabla_\theta L_b(\theta).
\label{eq:microbatch}
\tag{2}\end{equation}
Absolute products are computed after this signed accumulation. Averaging already-absolute microbatch scores would change the statistic. Our measurement concerns the gradient of mean data loss, not expected per-example saliency; opposing responses may cancel before aggregation.

For included kernel/head weights $W_l$, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
S_l&=\sum_{i\in W_l}a_i,\qquad T=\sum_lS_l,\\
q_l&=\frac{S_l}{T+\epsilon},\qquad\epsilon=10^{-12}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:shares}
\tag{3}\end{equation}
Biases are excluded from $T$ and measured separately. A record is invalid for nonfinite telemetry, $T=0$, or $\epsilon/(T+\epsilon)\geq0.01$. Probe calls use evaluation mode with gradients enabled, no optimiser update and preserved parameters, training mode and relevant random-number states. Full gradient tensors are discarded after summarisation.

\subsection{Architecture and allocation}
The convolutional neural network (CNN), CNN A, has three $3\times3$ padded convolutions with widths $3\!\rightarrow\!16\!\rightarrow\!32\!\rightarrow\!64$, trainable biases and rectified linear activations. The first two stages have $2\times2$ max pooling. Global average pooling feeds a biased $64\!\rightarrow\!10$ classifier. There is no batch normalisation or dropout. The shallow block contains conv1 kernels; middle contains conv2 kernels; deep combines conv3 kernels and classifier weights. Individual learned-layer totals and bias diagnostics remain available.

The distinction from norms is algebraically transparent. For one learned layer,
\begin{equation}
S_l=\langle|w_l|,|g_l|\rangle
\leq\|w_l\|_2\|g_l\|_2=U_l.
\label{eq:norm}
\tag{4}\end{equation}
Separate weight/gradient norms do not determine coordinate co-location. For example, weights (2,1) and gradients (2,1) or (1,2) have identical gradient marginals and norms but sensitivity totals five and four. This is an algebraic information distinction, not evidence of subgroup prediction. The matched norm-product and marginal-statistic controls below test whether that distinction adds useful forecast information under the implemented learning procedures.


\subsection{A falsifiable specificity argument}
For nonzero $U_l$ in (4), write $S_l=U_l c_l$, where
\begin{equation}
c_l=\frac{\langle|w_l|,|g_l|\rangle}{\|w_l\|_2\|g_l\|_2}\in[0,1].
\tag{5}\end{equation}
This elementary factorisation separates ordinary scale from the co-location of absolute coordinate magnitudes. It supplies a reason to measure allocation, but it does not prove that $c_l$ forecasts group failure. Likewise, a normalised profile is compositional: one block's larger share changes the others. The head, biases and shared denominator must therefore be examined before assigning a depth-specific interpretation.

Let X denote conventional/global inputs and Z the six L6 share summaries. A predictive increment from adding Z is conditional on the bounded learner and the input bank; it is not an estimate of conditional mutual information or a proof that no alternative statistic recovers the signal. We therefore repeat the addition after expanding X with norm-product allocation, parameter/mean-gradient marginals and shared bias diagnostics. The fresh MZ-versus-M result tests this conditional claim and does not demonstrate primary added value.

\subsection{Prefix features and nested learners}
At actual full-horizon fractions $t_1,\ldots,t_m$, each signal has endpoint, arithmetic mean, population standard deviation and ordinary least-squares slope
\begin{equation}
\widehat\beta(q)=\frac{\sum_j(t_j-\bar t)(q_j-\bar q)}{\sum_j(t_j-\bar t)^2}.
\label{eq:slope}
\tag{6}\end{equation}
Initialization is diagnostic and excluded from summaries. The 10\% prefix uses six fixed checkpoints; the secondary 5\% prefix uses its available checkpoints. Invalid observations are not interpolated.

The conventional predictor E is selected from compact/rich telemetry, with or without output separation, and two bounded learner classes. B adds four total-sensitivity summaries. L2 adds shallow/deep endpoint shares; L6 adds their means and population standard deviations; L8 adds two slopes. L6 therefore adds six shares, denoted Z. Endpoints are time-anchored, whereas means and spreads discard checkpoint order. L8 remains the original pilot primary candidate. The subsequent validation fixes L6 prospectively and includes L2 to distinguish endpoint-only from multi-checkpoint information; that comparison is secondary to the matched structural test.

\subsection{The audit decision}
For the same eligible candidate pool of $N$ classifiers, rank frozen failure probabilities in descending order, breaking ties by run identifier. Inspect $k=\lfloor0.25N\rfloor$ in the original primary experiment. Let $F$ be the observed number of failures and $D_A(k)$ the failures discovered by predictor A. Report precision $D_A/k$, recall $D_A/F$, random expected discoveries $kF/N$ and oracle ceiling $\min(k,F)$. Precision at $k=0$ is undefined. The comparison is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{20}(A,B)=\frac{20\,[D_A(k)-D_B(k)]}{N}.
\label{eq:gain}
\tag{7}\end{equation}
The original practical target was one extra failure per twenty candidates. Average precision, receiver-operating-characteristic area and Brier score are additional measures, not substitutes for discovery gain.

\subsection{Source construction and reservations}
The outcome study uses official SpuCoMNIST with one class per digit and RGB $28\times28$ inputs [16]. Its 24 setting families are the Cartesian product of alignment probabilities $0.10,0.50,0.80,0.95$, background difficulties MAGNITUDE\_LARGE/MAGNITUDE\_MEDIUM and Adam learning rates $0.0003,0.001,0.003$. Alignment denotes the fraction assigned the class-associated colour; ten-colour independence corresponds to $0.10$, not zero.

The fixed palette, digit identities, ordinary labels and no-noise/no-augmentation choices are verified. Inspection of the pinned generator established that LARGE modifies zero-background pixels over the image, while MEDIUM uses a $4\times4$ corner mask. The official implementation was checked for all 48 seeded source constructions; foreground pixels and labels were preserved. Each construction has 48,004 source-training identities: 256/class are reserved for panel A, another 256/class for B and 128/class for source health, leaving 41,604 training examples. Reservations use ordinary class labels only, with disjoint original MNIST identities and offline background metadata.

Each family has two source constructions and two initialisations per construction. Its four classifiers remain together through splitting, cross-validation and resampling. A configuration-only split with fixed seed 81001 allocates 14 families/56 runs to development and ten families/40 runs to evaluation, with every factor level represented on both sides. These are 24 setting families sharing MNIST, not 96 independent benchmark draws.

\subsection{Training and terminal outcomes}
All models train from scratch with Adam betas $(0.9,0.999)$, no weight decay, batch size 128, no dropped final batch and a constant learning rate. There are 326 updates per epoch, including a final four-example batch. The frozen full horizon is 60 epochs or 19,560 updates. Early observation steps are $196,391,587,978,1467,1956$, corresponding to rounded 1\%, 2\%, 3\%, 5\%, 7.5\% and 10\% fractions. Every non-diverged scientific model completes the full horizon; all 96 did so.

Source-normal eligibility is measured independently at 10\% on the health panel: accuracy at least $0.80$, mean cross-entropy at most $\ln(10)$ and finite required measurements. Ineligible models remain in the full-cohort analysis. The secondary 5\% predictions retain the same 10\%-defined cohort rather than selecting a new favourable population.

Development terminal labels use official validation identities, evaluation labels official test identities. A common outcome panel per difficulty/split uses fixed approximately balanced class/background assignments. Each model has 100 predefined digit/background groups, with observed group sizes 89--135. Define $\operatorname{WGA}=\min_g c_g/n_g$ and failure as $\operatorname{WGA}<0.75$; equality is non-failure. We retain group counts and marginal 95\% Wilson diagnostics. These are not simultaneous uncertainty bounds for the minimum. Fixed 0.70/0.80 sensitivity cutoffs and continuous worst-group regression are secondary. Missing groups, numerical failures and incompleteness are separate statuses, not silently converted to subgroup failures.

\subsection{Telemetry and forecaster selection}
The shared compact bank has learning rate, a fixed activation snapshot and four summaries of nine signals: loss, accuracy, worst-class accuracy, confidence, correct-only confidence, entropy, global mean-gradient norm, weight-norm growth and gradient signal-to-noise. Confidence is maximum softmax probability; entropy uses natural logarithms. Weight growth is $\|w_t\|_2/\|w_0\|_2-1$ over learned weight tensors. The activation snapshot records the nonpositive convolution preactivation fraction, equivalent to zero post-ReLU activations.

The gradient signal-to-noise adaptation uses a fixed layer-stratified subset $\mathcal I$ of up to 4,096 kernel/head coordinates and fixed probe microbatches:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{GNS}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal I|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\frac{|\bar g_i|}{s_i+10^{-12}},
\label{eq:gns}
\tag{8}\end{equation}
where $\bar g_i$ and $s_i$ are the mean and population deviation of that coordinate's microbatch gradients. This frozen-weight statistic differs from changing-weight epoch telemetry. Rich candidates give loss, accuracy, gradient signal-to-noise and weight growth ten summaries: first, last, change, mean, population deviation, minimum, maximum, area, slope and sign reversals.

The output-separation candidate clusters within-class probability vectors with $k=2$ or $3$, fixed k-means settings, positive silhouette and at least five samples per cluster; otherwise that class has one cluster. It records separation, smallest-cluster fraction and cluster accuracy gap. No group annotations determine these clusters or the observation time.

Regularised logistic regression uses $C\in\{0.1,1,10\}$. Histogram boosting uses depths 1/2/3, 100 iterations, rate 0.05, fixed seeds and disabled early stopping. The historical implementation retained the installed minimum leaf size of 20; this restricts splitting in some small grouped folds and is a comparison limitation. We report it rather than changing defaults after outcomes.

Development selection uses family-grouped out-of-fold discovery at 25\% capacity, average precision as a tie-break and the frozen deterministic candidate ordering. Three stratified grouped folds are preferred, with a two-fold fallback if needed; training and validation folds must contain both outcomes. Imputation, scaling and constant filtering are fit within training folds only. The chosen primary E is compact logistic telemetry without output-separation inputs. All sensitivity variants share this input bank, but their regularisation is selected separately (Table~2). This joint change in input set and fitted regularisation limits direct coefficient-level attribution.

Learning rate is allowed. Alignment, difficulty, generator seeds, subgroup annotations, family/run identifiers, future observations and terminal metrics are excluded from early predictors. A shared diagnostic adds bias-norm/sensitivity endpoints and slopes to E and L8. The selected non-temporal control is logistic B. A final-monitoring reference uses final ordinary health loss/accuracy and learning rate; it receives no terminal subgroup metrics as inputs.

\subsection{Outcome boundary and robustness analysis}
Design and family manifests precede collection. Development classifiers and validation outcomes precede predictor fitting. Saved pipelines, inputs and hyperparameters are frozen before held-out early risks; those risks are frozen before test outcomes are unsealed. The saved audit verifies this order and the hashes. The 21 outcome integration checks and complete-horizon audit support implementation correctness, not predictive superiority.

Panel A is primary. Secondary checks apply frozen A pipelines to panel B and use previously fitted B pipelines with A-selected choices. The post-hoc study loads these existing variants without refitting. It computes every capacity $k=0,\ldots,N$ and marks nine/twelve inspections; twelve is one third for the source-normal pool and 30\% for the full pool.

Uncertainty uses 2,000 paired draws of all ten held-out setting families, seed 130005, keeping nested runs together and recomputing pool size, ranking and fractional capacity. The historical empty/single-outcome exclusion rule is retained for interval comparability: 1,942 draws are valid and 58 invalid. Raw discovery counts remain defined for single-outcome pools. Percentile intervals are conditional on frozen forecasters and observed outcomes, exclude forecaster-selection uncertainty, and are pointwise rather than simultaneous evidence across a searched capacity curve.

Leave-one-family-out analysis removes each of the ten families, including the one with no eligible models. Fractions 1/4 and 1/3 are recomputed using the remaining pool. A family omission is an influence diagnostic, not an independent replication. The post-hoc stage reproduces 27 saved-prediction comparisons to maximum absolute error approximately $1.11\times10^{-16}$ and passes 16 diagnostic checks.

\subsection{Matched structural controls and fresh validation}

The saved-checkpoint study remeasures all 96 pilot classifiers at six checkpoints on both original panels. N adds six summaries of norm-product allocation: for each learned layer, U is its weight L2 norm times its mean-probe-gradient L2 norm; shallow/deep shares use the sum over conv1, conv2, conv3 and head plus the original stabiliser. NZ adds exactly Z. M extends N with weight and mean-gradient L2 norms plus mean absolute magnitude, signed population standard deviation, absolute median and absolute 90th percentile for each of those four layers, each summarised by endpoint, mean and population standard deviation. It also includes shared bias norm and bias sensitivity summaries. MZ adds exactly the same six Z columns; head and bias controls are shared, not exclusive to MZ.

Each pair uses identical cohort, checkpoints, panels, family folds, fold-local imputation/scaling/filtering, learner class and grid allowance. Logistic C is 0.1, 1 or 10. New histogram controls use depth 1/2/3, 100 iterations, rate 0.05, minimum leaf size five and disabled early stopping. This improves the small-fold comparator without altering historical predictions. Development out-of-fold discovery at one-third capacity selects each fit, with AP and frozen ordering as tie-breaks. Regularisation may differ between matched fits. The structural study is post hoc because pilot outcomes were already consumed; it cannot provide independent confirmation.

The fresh design has 64 new setting families: alignments 0.10/0.30/0.50/0.65/0.80/0.90/0.95/0.99, two background difficulties and learning rates 0.0002/0.0005/0.0015/0.004. A configuration-only split assigns 32 families each to development and evaluation; two constructions and two initialisations per family give 256 planned classifiers. Source reservations, 60-epoch horizon, measurement definitions, health and 0.75 failure rules follow the pilot. Five-fold grouped development CV is valid; the fallback is three folds. E and the strongest non-Z control are selected on development only. E selects the 50-input conventional bank with output separation; M/MZ have 186/192 inputs and both select logistic C=0.1. The rich M bank is the development-selected strongest non-Z control.

An initial fresh attempt stopped after 124 of 128 development classifiers completed: two fixed extreme-alignment source constructions failed the official group-presence assertion, making four trajectories unavailable. The amendment permits these documented preparation failures, retains all completed development classifiers and all planned settings/seeds, and applies the same policy to evaluation without replacement. Development outcomes were already known; the amended freeze precedes readiness checks and predictor fitting, and fitted predictors precede evaluation preflight/training. Early risks are frozen before one-time terminal test unsealing. The amendment does not relax support, feature banks, learner grids, outcome cutoffs or scientific benefit gates.

The fresh primary estimand is the logistic MZ-M difference in failures discovered at floor(N/3), normalised as Delta20 in (7), among source-normal evaluation candidates at 10\%. The quarter-capacity, other learners, 5\% prefix, full cohort and panel variants are secondary. The benefit gate requires Delta20 at least one, a positive lower 95\% family bound, superiority over global and development-selected strongest non-Z controls, positive MZ-M gains on both B variants and no negative family-omission difference. The one-third choice is pilot-informed but precedes fresh evaluation outcomes.

For ranking/probability evidence, identical whole-family draws are used for each paired AP, AUROC and Brier contrast, recomputing weighted empirical metrics with score ties. All ten pilot evaluation families are sampled in 2,000 draws (seed 130005); the structural study uses 2,000 draws (210212); the fresh study uses all 32 evaluation families in 5,000 draws (710005), including families with no eligible candidates. Multiplicities replicate every nested run together. Empty/single-label pools are excluded for the common metric set: 1,942 valid/58 invalid in each pilot-based analysis and 5,000 valid/zero invalid in the fresh analysis. Two-sided percentile intervals are pointwise and conditional on frozen forecasters, excluding selection uncertainty and shared-image sampling uncertainty. Ranking intervals were added retrospectively without fitting or tuning.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Engineering and measurement feasibility}
The original six-prefix experiment used a 20-epoch planned horizon (6,520 updates) and stopped at 652 updates, approximately two actual epochs. It passed 13 correctness checks and the separately labelled smoke test. The analytical fixture, finite-difference gates, unequal-microbatch accumulation, restricted rescaling, repeatability and monitoring neutrality provide direct checks of the intended statistic. Analytical tolerances are $\mathrm{atol}=10^{-9}$ and $\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-7}$ in float64; float32 accumulation checks use $10^{-6}$ and $10^{-4}$, respectively. No scientific tolerance was relaxed to obtain a pass. Raw slopes from this horizon and the later 60-epoch study are not pooled.

For retained feature $j$ over the six prefixes, sampling noise is $\mathrm{RMS}_r(f_{A,r,j}-f_{B,r,j})/\sqrt{2}$ and spread is the sample standard deviation of $(f_{A,r,j}+f_{B,r,j})/2$. The screen divides spread by the larger of noise and a numerical floor. Floors are $10^{-5}$ for level summaries and $2\times10^{-5}/(t_m-t_1)$ for slopes, raised if repeated-feature noise is larger. Zero-noise, flat and invalid cases are explicitly recorded; these ratios are descriptive engineering screens.

At the selected 256/class budget, the original 10\% deep-slope spread/noise ratio was 3.050141 against a provisional screen of three. Several level features also passed. Ratios compare six-run spread with panel-A/B sampling variation, not population significance. Only three original prefixes met source health. The targeted follow-up reproduced the original feature formulas exactly and remeasured shares to maximum discrepancy approximately $8.33\times10^{-17}$.

Twenty alternative class-stratified partitions recomputed gradients from the saved probe union. The predefined deep-slope target passed in 15/20, short of its prospective 16/20 screen; its ratio median was 3.833 and range 2.086--6.886. These overlapping partitions show sampling sensitivity within one finite pool. Common-denominator conv3, head and combined deep slopes passed 16, 14 and 15 partitions, respectively; convolution-only conv3 passed ten. For the measured partition errors, the mean squared error of the combined deep summary equals the conv3 and head mean squared errors plus twice their uncentred mean product. This product was positive in all partitions: errors reinforced, rather than cancelled, on average. This diagnostic concerns probe-panel disagreement, not the causal origin of subgroup failures. This supports careful aggregation analysis while leaving a layer-specific mechanism unresolved.

The calibration trajectories completed six actual epochs. Neither the two-, four- nor six-epoch observation point passed the unchanged health rule for all three seeds; at four and six epochs two seeds passed. The conditional fresh 18-prefix stage was consequently not launched. The later 60-epoch outcome PoC was explicitly exploratory rather than a validated calibration solution.

\section{Discussion and Scope of the Findings}
\label{sec:discussion}
The pilot yielded encouraging non-slope layer-summary rankings, with two extra discoveries at twelve inspections and consistent probe variants. The paired-family ranking intervals are wide, and one influential-family omission reverses that capacity comparison. The reused-outcome structural study gives only a panel-dependent MZ increment. In the fresh evaluation the primary MZ-M discovery difference is zero, with ranking and probability intervals spanning zero. The more complete evidence therefore does not establish a sensitivity-specific advantage.

The pilot two-slope extension reaches the fitted L8 estimator but does not improve its discovery curve over L6. This does not imply that all history is unhelpful. In the fresh study L6 finds three more failures than L2 at one-third capacity, but their AP/AUROC difference intervals include zero, and neither observation overcomes the matched primary tie. Endpoint, prefix-distribution and directional information remain distinct questions.

The strongest structural alternative is now an executed comparison. M includes conventional/global information, norm-product allocation, layer weight/mean-gradient marginals and shared bias diagnostics; MZ adds exactly Z. Separate regularisation selection is part of the matched learning procedure, not a pure coefficient intervention. M has 186 inputs relative to 107 eligible fresh development models, so dimensionality and bounded learners remain limitations. Nevertheless, the tested conditional specificity claim receives no primary support; simpler controls cannot be dismissed on the basis of the pilot.

The practical interpretation is similarly conditional. At the same number of audits the methods spend the same audit capacity; an increased yield is not automatically a monetary saving. Inexpensive benchmark group evaluation may favour auditing every classifier. A realistic use case requires a costly audit or an early reservation deadline, plus monitoring overhead that is justified by the value of additional discoveries. This study demonstrates a ranking decision and observation timing, not operational deployment savings.

No Waterbirds, pretrained-model transfer or other external benchmark result is presented. The fresh 256-planned-run study is within SpuCoMNIST, with 248 completed classifiers and eight data-unavailable trajectories. Prediction depth and full reproductions of related temporal/output methods remain omitted comparators. The improved nonlinear controls address the historical minimum-leaf limitation in the new studies, while the original pilot predictions remain unchanged.


\subsection{Implications of the completed validation}
The fresh protocol already performed the matched addition originally proposed for the next study. Implementation and evaluability gates passed, while the useful-gain, positive-interval and panel-consistency requirements failed. We retain that decision rather than promote a favourable learner, smaller control or secondary budget. The present evidence supports narrowing or revising the sensitivity-specific representation before further validation.

A materially revised representation needs its own rationale, matched structural controls, development selection and untouched evaluation. Reusing the present outcomes for feature design remains exploratory. A future external-context study must distinguish separately fitted replication of a learning procedure from direct transfer of a frozen predictor. Neither is established by this within-benchmark evaluation.

The one-third capacity was chosen using consumed pilot diagnostics but frozen before fresh evaluation outcomes. That order makes it the fresh study primary budget without making the earlier twelve-audit result prospective. The construction-availability amendment was made after development outcomes were known and before fresh evaluation training; it changed execution eligibility only, retained fixed identities and did not select replacements by performance.

Counts of training trajectories alone do not establish inferential precision. Readiness must assess outcome-bearing families, eligibility, valid grouped folds and measurement reliability without requiring a favourable layer advantage. The fresh study met those support requirements and still did not demonstrate the predefined benefit. More data or a second benchmark cannot be assumed to make the current representation succeed.

\subsection{Operational value as an explicit decision test}
Consider an intended workflow in which a team must reserve specialist audit time or commission a labelled evaluation panel before all candidate training runs finish. An early risk ranking could inform which candidates receive those reservations; historical audited runs would supply the training labels. This is a proposed workflow, not a deployment evaluated here. An early shortlist is useful when audit slots, specialist time or later data collection must be reserved before terminal outcomes are available. That is an operating scenario to validate, not an assumed property of this inexpensive benchmark. At equal audit count $k$, define a transparent utility scenario
\begin{equation}
\Delta U=v\,[D_{\mathrm{layer}}(k)-D_{\mathrm{base}}(k)]-\Delta C_m,
\tag{9}\end{equation}
where $v$ is an explicitly assumed value per discovered failure and $\Delta C_m$ is incremental monitoring cost. Shared training and equal-count audit costs cancel under this comparison. A positive observed discovery difference supplies a possible benefit region; it does not demonstrate monetary savings without measured cost and a justified value assumption. If exhaustive auditing is cheaper or the baseline reaches the oracle ceiling, the appropriate conclusion is limited operational headroom. This keeps scientific ranking value, early lead time and practical economics separately assessable.

\section{Conclusion}
GradientGuard provides a reproducible framework for measuring early layer-wise connection sensitivity and testing its value for subgroup-audit prioritisation. The exploratory pilot shows favourable non-slope ranking point estimates but an original primary discovery tie and wide paired-family uncertainty. Matched norm-product and rich structural controls weaken a sensitivity-specific interpretation. In the separately frozen fresh-family validation, MZ and M both discover 26 failures in 35 audits among 106 source-normal classifiers; ranking/probability difference intervals include zero. Smaller-control and secondary gains remain bounded observations. The completed evidence establishes measurement correctness and a transparent negative primary specificity result within SpuCoMNIST, without demonstrating causal shortcut detection, cross-benchmark transfer or operational savings.


\section{Analytical Fixture and Evidence Access}
\label{app:fixture}
The fixture embeds one active channel through all three convolutions. Each active convolution uses a unit centre-kernel weight, and a unit classifier weight connects the pooled channel to one logit. Other connecting weights are zero; negative biases keep unused channels inactive. Unique pooling maxima and a twice-pooled spatial average of one avoid ambiguity. Bias states $(0,0,0,0)$ and $(0.5,-0.75,0,0.25)$ give selected logit one and the same total weight sensitivity $4|r|$, where $r$ is the selected-logit loss derivative. Their four learned-layer sensitivities divided by $|r|$ are $(1,1,1,1)$ and $(1,1.5,0.75,0.75)$, with ideal three-block shares $(0.25,0.25,0.50)$ and $(0.25,0.375,0.375)$. Epsilon is included in implementation comparisons. This validates possible profile information in CNN A, not a subgroup-accuracy difference or an empirical mechanism.
